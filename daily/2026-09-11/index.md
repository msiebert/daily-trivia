  - id: "2026-09-11-1"
    question: "What reddish mineral, worn from Antarctica's bedrock and frozen into the base of ice shelves, gives some Antarctic icebergs their eerie emerald-green color instead of the usual blue-white?"
    answer: "Iron oxide"
    topic: nature
    explanation: >-
      For decades, sailors and scientists occasionally spotted brilliant
      emerald-green icebergs in Antarctic waters, but no one could explain
      why, since pure glacial ice normally filters sunlight down to a cool
      blue. A 2019 study led by University of Washington researcher Stephen
      Warren found the answer in an ice core from the Amery Ice Shelf: near
      its base, iron levels ran roughly 500 times higher than in the glacier
      ice above. That iron comes from "glacial flour," rock ground to
      powder as ice sheets scrape over bedrock, which washes into the sea
      and refreezes onto the underside of ice shelves. When such a layer
      calves off, its iron oxide absorbs blue light while the ice itself
      absorbs red, letting green light escape.
  - id: "2026-09-11-2"
    question: "In what year was the International Ice Patrol established to track North Atlantic icebergs, less than a year after the Titanic disaster?"
    answer: "1913"
    topic: nature
    explanation: >-
      When Titanic sank in April 1912 after striking an iceberg, it exposed
      how little systematic monitoring existed for ice hazards along
      shipping lanes. Maritime nations responded quickly, founding the
      International Ice Patrol in 1913, initially run by the U.S. Revenue
      Cutter Service and later the U.S. Coast Guard, to survey iceberg
      positions each ice season and broadcast warnings to ships. It has
      operated continuously except during the World Wars, and today combines
      aerial reconnaissance, satellite imagery, and ocean modeling to
      forecast how far icebergs will drift into transatlantic shipping
      routes.
  - id: "2026-09-11-3"
    question: "The largest iceberg ever reliably recorded, B-15, calved from Antarctica's Ross Ice Shelf in 2000 with a surface area comparable in size to which Caribbean island nation?"
    answer: "Jamaica"
    topic: nature
    explanation: >-
      Iceberg B-15 calved from the Ross Ice Shelf in March 2000 and, at
      roughly 295 by 37 kilometers with a surface area around 11,000 square
      kilometers, remains the largest iceberg ever reliably measured,
      comparable in area to the entire island of Jamaica. Weighing an
      estimated 3 billion tons of freshwater ice, it was likely part of a
      natural calving cycle that recurs on the Ross Ice Shelf every 50 to
      100 years. Rather than melting quickly, it fractured into large
      pieces over the following years; the biggest fragment, B-15A, itself
      covered around 6,400 square kilometers and drifted for years, at
      times blocking penguin colonies' access to open water near McMurdo
      Sound.
