  - id: "2026-09-13-1"
    question: "Frederick Banting, Charles Best, and James Collip patented their insulin extraction process in 1923, then sold that patent to the University of Toronto for what token sum, declaring the discovery belonged to the world?"
    answer: "$1"
    topic: science
    explanation: >-
      Worried that a monopoly on insulin's production would put it out of
      reach for the diabetic patients who desperately needed it, the three
      researchers who held the extraction patent assigned it to the
      University of Toronto for a single symbolic dollar apiece. Banting
      reportedly said insulin did not belong to him but belonged to the
      world, and the university then licensed manufacturers widely rather
      than controlling supply itself. The gesture is still cited as a
      landmark case in the ethics of pharmaceutical patents, and it stands
      in sharp contrast to modern debates over the high price of
      insulin products a century later.
  - id: "2026-09-13-2"
    question: "What 14-year-old Toronto boy became the first person ever treated with insulin, in January 1922, surviving an impurity-triggered allergic reaction to the first injection before a more purified second dose brought his blood sugar down within a day?"
    answer: "Leonard Thompson"
    topic: science
    explanation: >-
      Leonard Thompson, a 14-year-old dying of type 1 diabetes at
      Toronto General Hospital, received the first-ever insulin injection
      on January 11, 1922. That initial extract was too impure, and
      Thompson developed an abscess and an allergic reaction with little
      improvement in his condition. Biochemist James Collip spent the next
      twelve days refining the extraction process, and a second, far
      purer injection on January 23 brought Thompson's dangerously high
      blood sugar down to normal within a single day. He lived another
      thirteen years on insulin before dying of an unrelated illness, one
      of the first of millions of lives the treatment would go on to save.
  - id: "2026-09-13-3"
    question: "When the 1923 Nobel Prize for insulin went only to Frederick Banting and John Macleod, excluding two key collaborators, Banting protested by splitting his prize money with which graduate student?"
    answer: "Charles Best"
    topic: science
    explanation: >-
      The 1923 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine credited only
      Frederick Banting and lab director John Macleod for insulin's
      discovery, overlooking Charles Best and James Collip, whose lab work
      had been essential to actually isolating and purifying it. Furious
      that Best had been left out, Banting announced he would share half
      of his own prize money with him; Macleod, in turn, split his half
      with Collip. The snub was controversial enough that in 1972 the
      Nobel Foundation revised the official language describing the award,
      acknowledging the broader team's role in turning insulin into a
      usable medical treatment.
