  - id: "2026-09-15-1"
    question: "What painting by Leonardo da Vinci, housed in Paris's Louvre Museum and famous for its subject's enigmatic half-smile, is widely considered the most famous artwork in the world?"
    answer: "Mona Lisa"
    topic: famous-people
    explanation: >-
      Painted in the early 1500s, the portrait likely depicts Lisa
      Gherardini, the wife of a Florentine silk merchant, though Leonardo
      kept working on it for years and reportedly never delivered it to
      the family that commissioned it. Its soft, almost smoky transitions
      between light and shadow come from a technique called sfumato,
      which Leonardo pioneered to avoid harsh outlines. The painting's
      fame exploded after it was stolen from the Louvre in 1911 and
      recovered two years later, turning it into a global media
      sensation. Today it draws millions of visitors a year and hangs
      behind bulletproof glass as the Louvre's single most-viewed work.
  - id: "2026-09-15-2"
    question: "What term describes the reversed, right-to-left script Leonardo da Vinci used throughout his personal notebooks, legible only when held up to a reflective surface?"
    answer: "Mirror writing"
    topic: famous-people
    explanation: >-
      Leonardo was left-handed, and writing right-to-left in reversed
      letters kept his hand from dragging through wet ink as it moved
      across the page. He used this script for the vast majority of his
      roughly 13,000 surviving notebook pages, covering everything from
      anatomy to engineering to painting theory. Historians still debate
      whether it was purely a practical habit or also a way to keep his
      notes private from casual readers. Modern transcriptions and
      digitized scans let scholars study the pages today without needing
      an actual mirror.
  - id: "2026-09-15-3"
    question: "Leonardo da Vinci's notebooks include an early design, often nicknamed the 'aerial screw,' for what flying machine that wasn't actually built and flown for real until centuries later?"
    answer: "Helicopter"
    topic: famous-people
    explanation: >-
      Sketched around 1489, the design shows a spiral-shaped surface
      meant to compress air and generate lift, turned by several men
      pushing a central shaft. Leonardo never built or tested it, and
      engineers today generally agree the materials and power available
      at the time couldn't have gotten it off the ground. Even so, it's
      widely cited as one of the earliest conceptual ancestors of the
      modern helicopter. It sits alongside other flying-machine studies in
      his notebooks, including designs inspired by the wings of birds and
      bats.
