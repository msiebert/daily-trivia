  - id: "2026-09-16-1"
    question: "What German inventor introduced the printing press with movable metal type to Europe around 1440?"
    answer: "Johannes Gutenberg"
    topic: technology
    explanation: >-
      Johannes Gutenberg was a goldsmith from Mainz, Germany, who adapted
      techniques from metalworking, winemaking, and existing screw presses
      to build a machine that could reproduce text far faster than hand
      copying or woodblock printing. His key innovation was casting
      individual, reusable letters out of a durable metal alloy, letting
      typesetters arrange and rearrange them for each new page. The
      resulting printing press is widely credited with triggering a
      revolution in the spread of literacy, scientific ideas, and religious
      reform across Europe within just a few decades.
  - id: "2026-09-16-2"
    question: "What is the name of the elaborately illustrated Bible, completed around 1455, that stands as the first major book produced using the printing press?"
    answer: "Gutenberg Bible"
    topic: technology
    explanation: >-
      Printed in Latin in Gutenberg's workshop in Mainz, this Bible took
      several years to complete and used two columns of dense, uniform
      type per page, closely mimicking the look of a hand-copied manuscript
      to appeal to buyers used to that style. Around 180 copies are
      believed to have been printed, some on paper and some on more
      expensive vellum, and roughly 49 copies or partial copies survive
      today in libraries and museums around the world. Its craftsmanship
      is still considered a technical high point of early printing, not
      merely a first attempt.
  - id: "2026-09-16-3"
    question: "About how many copies of the Bible did Johannes Gutenberg's printing press produce in its first run, a dramatic leap in output compared to hand-copied manuscripts?"
    answer: "180"
    topic: technology
    explanation: >-
      Before the printing press, producing a single Bible by hand could
      take a scribe a year or more, making books rare and expensive luxury
      items. Gutenberg's press, by contrast, produced roughly 180 copies of
      his Bible in a matter of a few years using a small workshop crew,
      an output increase that seems modest today but was staggering by the
      standards of the 1450s. That leap in production speed is a major
      reason movable-type printing spread to dozens of European cities
      within just a few decades of Gutenberg's invention.
