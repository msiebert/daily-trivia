  - id: "2026-09-18-1"
    question: "What hero avoided Medusa's deadly, petrifying stare by looking at her only as a reflection in a polished bronze shield before beheading her?"
    answer: "Perseus"
    topic: mythology
    explanation: >-
      Perseus was sent to fetch Medusa's head as what was meant to be an
      impossible, fatal task, but the gods equipped him for it: Hades lent
      him a cap of invisibility, Hermes gave him winged sandals, and
      Athena provided a polished bronze shield. Rather than look directly
      at Medusa, whose gaze turned any onlooker to stone, Perseus tracked
      her only through her mirrored reflection in the shield while she
      slept, then struck off her head in a single blow. He carried the
      severed head away in a special pouch, since it remained lethal even
      after death, and later used it as a weapon against his enemies. The
      episode became one of the most frequently depicted scenes in Greek
      art, from ancient vase paintings to Renaissance sculpture.
  - id: "2026-09-18-2"
    question: "What winged horse leaped fully grown from Medusa's neck the instant Perseus struck off her head?"
    answer: "Pegasus"
    topic: mythology
    explanation: >-
      When Perseus beheaded Medusa, two offspring of her union with
      Poseidon sprang from her neck fully formed: the winged horse Pegasus
      and the giant warrior Chrysaor. Pegasus went on to have his own
      career in Greek myth, most famously carrying the hero Bellerophon
      into battle against the fire-breathing Chimera before Bellerophon's
      later fall from grace. The image of a winged horse emerging from a
      monster's severed neck is one of the most enduring visual motifs
      from the whole Perseus myth, and it's the reason Pegasus is so
      tightly linked to Medusa in art and popular retellings even though
      the two rarely appear together while she's alive.
  - id: "2026-09-18-3"
    question: "Which goddess cursed the once-beautiful Medusa with snake hair and a petrifying gaze after Poseidon assaulted her inside that goddess's own temple?"
    answer: "Athena"
    topic: mythology
    explanation: >-
      In the version of the myth popularized by the Roman poet Ovid,
      Medusa was originally a beautiful mortal woman before Poseidon
      assaulted her inside one of Athena's temples. Rather than punishing
      Poseidon, Athena turned her anger on Medusa, transforming her hair
      into venomous snakes and cursing her with a gaze that turned anyone
      who met it to stone. This framing recasts Medusa less as a monster
      born evil and more as a victim of divine injustice, a reading that
      has become increasingly prominent in modern art, literature, and
      feminist retellings of the myth.
