  - id: "2026-09-21-1"
    question: "According to a well-known legend, what Ethiopian goat herder is said to have discovered coffee after noticing his goats grow unusually energetic from eating a certain shrub's red berries?"
    answer: "Kaldi"
    topic: food
    explanation: >-
      As the popular (though unverified) legend goes, a goat herder named
      Kaldi in Ethiopia's Kaffa region noticed his goats becoming
      unusually lively and unable to sleep after grazing on berries from a
      certain shrub. He tried the berries himself, felt the same jolt, and
      reportedly shared his find with local monks, who began brewing them
      into a drink to stay alert during long nights of prayer. Whether or
      not the story is literally true, it reflects Ethiopia's real
      historical role as coffee's birthplace, from which cultivation
      spread to Yemen and the wider Arab world by the 15th century.
  - id: "2026-09-21-2"
    question: "What natural stimulant in coffee beans is the most widely consumed psychoactive substance on Earth?"
    answer: "Caffeine"
    topic: food
    explanation: >-
      Caffeine occurs naturally in coffee, tea, cacao, and a handful of
      other plants, and it works by blocking adenosine receptors in the
      brain, the very receptors that normally build up the feeling of
      drowsiness. Because it's legal, cheap, and consumed daily by most
      adults worldwide, researchers routinely describe it as the most
      widely used psychoactive drug on the planet. It wasn't chemically
      isolated until 1819, when German chemist Friedlieb Ferdinand Runge
      extracted it from coffee beans -- more than a thousand years after
      people first started drinking the stuff.
  - id: "2026-09-21-3"
    question: "What South American country produces more coffee than any other nation, growing roughly a third to two-fifths of the world's entire supply?"
    answer: "Brazil"
    topic: food
    explanation: >-
      Brazil has been the world's top coffee grower for generations,
      thanks to vast, sun-drenched highland plantations well suited to
      growing beans at scale. Its annual harvest regularly tops 60 million
      60-kilogram bags, accounting for somewhere between a third and two
      fifths of global production depending on the year's weather and
      harvest size. Vietnam is a distant second, specializing heavily in
      the hardier robusta bean rather than the milder arabica that
      dominates Brazilian exports. Together, the two countries alone grow
      more than half the world's coffee.
