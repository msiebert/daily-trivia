  - id: "2026-09-22-1"
    question: "The Sahara, the world's largest hot desert, stretches across how many countries in North Africa, from Morocco to Sudan?"
    answer: "11"
    topic: nature
    explanation: >-
      The Sahara covers roughly 3.6 million square miles -- an area close
      to the size of the United States or China -- and spans 11 countries
      including Morocco, Algeria, Libya, Egypt, Chad, and Sudan. Despite
      its reputation as an endless sea of sand, only about a quarter of
      that area is actually dunes; the rest is barren rocky plateau,
      gravel plains, dry mountains, and salt flats. It's also the
      hottest desert on Earth, with an average annual temperature around
      86°F and recorded highs above 130°F, even though nighttime
      temperatures can plunge below freezing once the sun goes down.
  - id: "2026-09-22-2"
    question: "About how many thousand years ago did the 'Green Sahara' -- a millennia-long stretch when the desert was grassland dotted with lakes -- dry up into the desert seen today?"
    answer: "5,000"
    topic: nature
    explanation: >-
      From roughly 11,000 to 5,000 years ago, what's now the Sahara was
      savanna and wooded grassland, receiving up to ten times today's
      rainfall and supporting permanent lakes -- including Lake Mega
      Chad, which at its peak covered more than 400,000 square
      kilometers, along with hunter-gatherer communities. The shift was
      driven by a slow wobble in Earth's axis called precession, which
      periodically strengthens the West African monsoon; scientists have
      traced over 230 of these "greenings" occurring roughly every
      21,000 years over the past eight million years. When the monsoon
      weakened again around 5,000 years ago, the region dried out into
      the desert known today.
  - id: "2026-09-22-3"
    question: "What single valley in Chad, a dried-out lakebed packed with phosphorus-rich dust, supplies roughly half of all the Sahara dust that fertilizes the Amazon rainforest each year?"
    answer: "Bodele Depression"
    topic: nature
    explanation: >-
      The Bodele Depression is a mountain-rimmed valley in Chad that
      covers only about 0.2% of the Sahara's total area, yet a natural
      wind tunnel between the Tibesti and Ennedi mountain ranges funnels
      dust out of it so intensely that it supplies roughly half of the
      roughly 40 million tons of Saharan dust that crosses the Atlantic
      to the Amazon basin each year. That dust, rich in phosphorus from
      the ancient lakebed's dead microorganisms, replenishes nutrients
      that heavy tropical rains constantly wash out of Amazonian soil --
      making a single Saharan valley an unlikely lifeline for a
      rainforest thousands of miles away.
