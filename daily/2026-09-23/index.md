  - id: "2026-09-23-1"
    question: "What bacterium, transmitted mainly through flea bites, caused the pandemic known as the Black Death that swept through Europe between 1347 and 1351?"
    answer: "Yersinia pestis"
    topic: history
    explanation: >-
      Yersinia pestis normally lives in wild rodent populations, and the
      Black Death is thought to have spread to humans after fleas that
      had fed on infected rats began biting people once their rodent
      hosts died off. The bacterium causes three related illnesses --
      bubonic plague, which swells the lymph nodes into painful
      "buboes"; septicemic plague, which poisons the blood; and
      pneumonic plague, which spreads through the air between people and
      is almost always fatal without treatment. Modern DNA testing on
      teeth pulled from mass graves in Europe has confirmed Yersinia
      pestis as the pandemic's cause, settling a debate that once
      included rival theories about anthrax or a mystery virus.
  - id: "2026-09-23-2"
    question: "In October 1347, Genoese trading ships carrying the Black Death docked at which Sicilian port, giving the plague its first foothold in Europe?"
    answer: "Messina"
    topic: history
    explanation: >-
      Twelve Genoese galleys returning from trade posts on the Black Sea
      pulled into Messina's harbor with most of their crews already dead
      or dying, and the survivors covered in strange black boils.
      Horrified locals drove the "death ships" back out to sea, but it
      was already too late -- the disease had reached shore, and within
      weeks it was spreading north through Sicily and across the
      Mediterranean. From that single port, the plague fanned out along
      Europe's dense network of trade routes, reaching Genoa and Venice
      within months and much of the continent within about three years.
  - id: "2026-09-23-3"
    question: "The Black Death is estimated to have killed roughly how many million people in Europe during its four-year rampage from 1347 to 1351?"
    answer: "25 million"
    topic: history
    explanation: >-
      Historians estimate the initial wave of the Black Death killed
      around 25 million people in Europe alone, roughly 30 to 60% of the
      continent's population at the time, with tens of millions more
      dying across Asia, North Africa, and the Middle East. Whole
      villages were abandoned, and the sudden labor shortage that
      followed helped break down Europe's feudal system, as surviving
      peasants could demand higher wages and greater freedom from
      landowners desperate for workers. Europe's population is not
      thought to have recovered to its pre-plague level until around
      1550, some two centuries after the first outbreak.
