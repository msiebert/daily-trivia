  - id: "2026-09-24-1"
    question: "Which Russian chemist published the first widely adopted periodic table in 1869, leaving gaps in it to predict properties of elements not yet discovered?"
    answer: "Dmitri Mendeleev"
    topic: science
    explanation: >-
      In 1869, while writing a chemistry textbook, Dmitri Mendeleev
      arranged the 63 known elements by atomic weight and noticed their
      properties repeated in a regular pattern. Where the pattern didn't
      fit an existing element, he trusted the pattern over convention and
      left blank spaces rather than forcing elements into the wrong slot.
      He then predicted the atomic weight, density, and chemical behavior
      of the missing elements before anyone had found them. When gallium,
      scandium, and germanium turned up over the following two decades
      and matched his forecasts closely, his table won over skeptical
      chemists worldwide, and element 101 was later named mendelevium in
      his honor.
  - id: "2026-09-24-2"
    question: "What element, discovered in France in 1875, matched the properties Mendeleev had forecast years earlier for an undiscovered element he called 'eka-aluminium'?"
    answer: "Gallium"
    topic: science
    explanation: >-
      Mendeleev's 1869 table left a gap beneath aluminum, and he
      predicted the missing element's atomic weight and density before
      anyone had isolated it. French chemist Paul-Emile Lecoq de
      Boisbaudran discovered gallium in 1875 by analyzing a zinc ore
      sample with a spectroscope, unaware of Mendeleev's prediction. When
      Mendeleev read about the discovery, he wrote pointing out that the
      measured density seemed off from his forecast, and a more careful
      remeasurement of the newly purified metal proved him right. The
      close match between prediction and discovery convinced the
      scientific community that the periodic table reflected a real law
      of nature, not just a convenient filing system.
  - id: "2026-09-24-3"
    question: "As of a 2016 announcement confirming four new element names, how many chemical elements make up today's completed periodic table?"
    answer: "118"
    topic: science
    explanation: >-
      Mendeleev's original table ranked elements by atomic weight, but in
      1913 physicist Henry Moseley showed via X-ray spectroscopy that
      atomic number -- the count of protons in an atom's nucleus -- was
      the true organizing principle, fixing a handful of anomalies in the
      old ordering. Scientists kept synthesizing new, superheavy elements
      in laboratories over the following century, adding each one as it
      was confirmed. In November 2016, the International Union of Pure
      and Applied Chemistry approved the last four names -- nihonium,
      moscovium, tennessine, and oganesson -- completing the table's
      seventh row and bringing the total to 118 confirmed elements, where
      it still stands.
