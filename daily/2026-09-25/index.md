  - id: "2026-09-25-1"
    question: "About how many miles long is Arizona's Grand Canyon, stretching from end to end?"
    answer: "277"
    topic: geography
    explanation: >-
      The Grand Canyon runs roughly 277 miles along the Colorado River
      through northern Arizona, making it one of the largest canyons on
      Earth by sheer scale. It's up to 18 miles wide in places and drops
      more than a mile -- about 6,093 feet at its deepest point -- from
      rim to river. Its exposed rock layers record nearly two billion
      years of Earth's geologic history, stacked in bands that are
      visible from the rim. That combination of length, depth, and
      visible geologic time is why it's often named among the Seven
      Natural Wonders of the World.
  - id: "2026-09-25-2"
    question: "What North American river carved the Grand Canyon over roughly the past 5 to 6 million years, and still flows through its base today?"
    answer: "Colorado River"
    topic: geography
    explanation: >-
      Geologists generally date the canyon's modern down-cutting to
      somewhere in the last 5 to 6 million years, as the river's current
      carved through layer after layer of sedimentary rock. The river's
      steep gradient and heavy sediment load, combined with the gradual
      uplift of the Colorado Plateau, let it erode far faster and deeper
      than most rivers manage. Today the same river still runs along the
      canyon floor, though the Glen Canyon Dam upstream now regulates its
      flow far more than nature once did. The canyon's rock layers below
      the rim are older still, with the oldest exposed at the bottom
      dating back nearly two billion years.
  - id: "2026-09-25-3"
    question: "In what year did President Woodrow Wilson sign the act designating the Grand Canyon a U.S. National Park?"
    answer: "1919"
    topic: geography
    explanation: >-
      Congress had considered protecting the canyon for decades -- Theodore
      Roosevelt declared it a national monument in 1908, calling it a
      sight every American should see -- but full national park status
      didn't arrive until Woodrow Wilson signed the designation into law
      on February 26, 1919. It became just the 17th site added to the
      U.S. National Park System. Six decades later, in 1979, UNESCO named
      it a World Heritage Site in recognition of its geologic and scenic
      significance. Today it draws roughly six million visitors a year,
      making it one of the most visited national parks in the country.
