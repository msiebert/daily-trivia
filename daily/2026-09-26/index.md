  - id: "2026-09-26-1"
    question: "What was Genghis Khan's birth name, before a 1206 gathering of Mongol chieftains proclaimed him ruler of a newly unified Mongol nation and granted him that title?"
    answer: "Temüjin"
    topic: famous-people
    explanation: >-
      Genghis Khan was born Temüjin sometime in the 1160s on the
      Mongolian steppe, the son of a minor clan chief who was poisoned
      by rivals while Temüjin was still a boy. He spent his youth in
      poverty and even captivity before slowly rebuilding alliances and
      defeating rival confederations such as the Tatars, Kereyid, and
      Naiman one by one. In 1206, a kurultai -- a council of Mongol
      chieftains -- gathered and proclaimed him ruler of a single
      unified Mongol nation, granting him the title Genghis Khan, often
      translated as "universal ruler." That moment marked the formal
      founding of what would grow into the Mongol Empire.
  - id: "2026-09-26-2"
    question: "Built from the conquests of Genghis Khan and the descendants who expanded on his work, roughly how many square miles did the Mongol Empire span at its peak, making it the largest contiguous land empire in history?"
    answer: "9 million"
    topic: famous-people
    explanation: >-
      Genghis Khan's campaigns fused the Mongol tribes into a single
      fighting force that, under him and the sons and grandsons who
      followed, conquered territory stretching from the Pacific coast of
      China to Eastern Europe and the Middle East. At its greatest
      extent the empire covered roughly 9 million square miles, making
      it the largest contiguous land empire ever assembled -- contiguous
      meaning connected by land rather than scattered across oceans,
      which is how the later British Empire could claim more total
      territory without breaking that record. Trade and communication
      across this vast, unified territory became so reliable that
      historians call the period the Pax Mongolica. Much of that peak
      size was reached generations after Genghis Khan's death, but the
      conquests and administrative system he built made it possible.
  - id: "2026-09-26-3"
    question: "Genghis Khan's grave has never been located because he asked to be buried without any marker -- tradition places his secret burial site near what sacred mountain in Mongolia?"
    answer: "Burkhan Khaldun"
    topic: famous-people
    explanation: >-
      When Genghis Khan died in 1227 during a campaign against the
      Western Xia kingdom, his followers carried out his wish to be
      buried without any monument, marker, or public ceremony. Tradition
      places his grave somewhere near Burkhan Khaldun, a sacred mountain
      in Mongolia's Khentii range that he had revered since childhood,
      and the surrounding region has been treated as forbidden ground
      for centuries. Even Marco Polo, writing decades later, noted that
      Mongols of his own time didn't know exactly where their great khan
      was buried. Despite modern searches using satellite imagery and
      other technology, the tomb has never been found, making it one of
      history's most enduring archaeological mysteries.
