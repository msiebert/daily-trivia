  - id: "2026-09-30-1"
    question: "What nuclear process converts hydrogen into helium deep in the Sun's core, releasing the energy that eventually reaches Earth as sunlight?"
    answer: "Fusion"
    topic: space
    explanation: >-
      Deep in the Sun's core, immense pressure and temperatures of about 15
      million degrees Celsius force hydrogen nuclei to fuse together into
      helium, a reaction called nuclear fusion. Each fusion reaction
      converts a tiny amount of mass directly into energy, following
      Einstein's famous equation E=mc². That energy takes an astonishingly
      long time -- tens of thousands of years -- to random-walk its way out
      from the core to the Sun's surface as photons. Once it escapes,
      though, that same light takes just over eight minutes to cross the 93
      million miles to Earth. Fusion is also the process scientists are
      racing to replicate in reactors on Earth, hoping to harness the same
      nearly limitless energy source that powers every star.
  - id: "2026-09-30-2"
    question: "What name is given to the Sun's outer atmosphere, a wispy layer that reaches temperatures of several million degrees -- hundreds of times hotter than the visible surface it surrounds, a temperature jump that still puzzles physicists?"
    answer: "Corona"
    topic: space
    explanation: >-
      The corona is the Sun's outermost atmospheric layer, visible to the
      naked eye as a pale halo during a total solar eclipse. Oddly, it can
      reach temperatures of a few million degrees Celsius, while the
      visible surface just beneath it, the photosphere, sits at a
      comparatively cool 5,500 degrees Celsius. This temperature jump
      remains one of solar physics' enduring mysteries, with leading
      theories pointing to magnetic waves and small-scale explosive events
      called nanoflares. The corona is also the source of the solar wind, a
      constant stream of charged particles flowing outward through the
      solar system.
  - id: "2026-09-30-3"
    question: "About how many minutes does it take sunlight to travel the roughly 93 million miles from the Sun to Earth?"
    answer: "8"
    topic: space
    explanation: >-
      Light travels at about 186,000 miles per second, and the Sun sits an
      average of 93 million miles from Earth -- a distance defined as one
      astronomical unit. Covering that gap takes sunlight about 8 minutes
      and 20 seconds, meaning whenever you look at the Sun, you're seeing
      it as it appeared more than eight minutes earlier. If the Sun
      suddenly vanished, Earth would keep orbiting and receiving its light
      for those same eight-plus minutes before anyone noticed. That delay
      is also why solar flares and other events on the Sun are always
      observed on Earth with a built-in lag.
