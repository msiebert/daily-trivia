  - id: "2026-10-01-1"
    question: "What critically endangered salamander, found in the wild only in a handful of canals around Mexico City, spends its entire life underwater with feathery gills instead of growing into a land-walking adult?"
    answer: "Axolotl"
    topic: animals
    explanation: >-
      The axolotl is a salamander native to the shallow lake and canal
      system of Xochimilco, on the southern edge of Mexico City, with wild
      populations also once found in nearby Lake Chalco before it was
      drained. Unlike most amphibians, it never leaves the water or
      develops lungs-first adult features -- it keeps its feathery external
      gills and aquatic body for its whole life. Urban sprawl, polluted
      canals, and invasive fish have pushed the wild population to the
      brink, with some surveys finding only a few dozen to a few hundred
      individuals left in their native habitat. Axolotls are far from rare
      in captivity, though -- they're bred by the millions as pets and lab
      animals, making them one of the few species that are simultaneously
      critically endangered in the wild and extremely common worldwide.
  - id: "2026-10-01-2"
    question: "What word names an axolotl's standout ability to regrow an entire missing limb -- bone, muscle, skin and all -- without leaving any scar behind?"
    answer: "Regeneration"
    topic: animals
    explanation: >-
      Axolotls can regrow not just limbs but also their tails, spinal
      cord, jaw, skin, and even parts of the heart and brain, rebuilding
      every layer of tissue with no scar tissue at all. A severed limb
      regrows through a structure called a blastema, a mass of cells at
      the wound site that essentially re-runs embryonic development to
      rebuild the missing part piece by piece. Researchers have amputated
      the same limb from a single axolotl multiple times and watched it
      regrow perfectly each time. Because mammals heal wounds with scar
      tissue instead of true regeneration, axolotls are one of the most
      closely studied animals in regenerative medicine, in hopes of one day
      unlocking similar healing in humans.
  - id: "2026-10-01-3"
    question: "What term describes an axolotl's habit of keeping its larval gills and aquatic body for its whole life, skipping the metamorphosis into a land-dwelling adult that most other salamanders go through?"
    answer: "Neoteny"
    topic: animals
    explanation: >-
      Most salamanders start life as gilled, water-bound larvae and then
      metamorphose into air-breathing adults that can live on land.
      Axolotls, by contrast, reach full adult size and sexual maturity
      while still looking and living like larvae -- a trait called
      neoteny. It's driven by unusually low levels of thyroid hormone,
      and it's not fully fixed: dosing a wild-type axolotl with the right
      hormone can actually force it through metamorphosis into a
      land-going form, though captive axolotls rarely encounter the
      conditions that would trigger this naturally. Neoteny is one of the
      main reasons axolotls became a favorite lab animal in the first
      place, since it gives researchers a rare window into an amphibian
      that stays in a juvenile, highly regenerative state for its entire
      life.
