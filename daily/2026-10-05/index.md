  - id: "2026-10-05-1"
    question: "What 8-year-old boy did English physician Edward Jenner inoculate with cowpox matter in May 1796, then deliberately expose to smallpox two months later to test whether the boy was protected?"
    answer: "James Phipps"
    topic: science
    explanation: >-
      On May 14, 1796, Jenner inoculated Phipps with pus taken from a cowpox lesion on the hand of
      dairymaid Sarah Nelmes. That July, Jenner deliberately exposed the boy to smallpox matter, and
      Phipps did not fall ill, demonstrating that cowpox exposure could confer protection. The
      experiment is widely credited as the founding demonstration of vaccination, though Jenner was
      not actually the first to notice or exploit cowpox's protective effect — farmers and at least
      one earlier experimenter had already tried it informally. His real contribution was turning
      that folk observation into a rigorous, published, repeatable scientific method.
  - id: "2026-10-05-2"
    question: "What Latin word for 'cow' gave us the word 'vaccine,' after Edward Jenner's cowpox-based method against smallpox was dubbed 'variolae vaccinae' -- 'smallpox of the cow'?"
    answer: "Vacca"
    topic: science
    explanation: >-
      Jenner's technique took its Latin name from the cowpox disease he used to trigger immunity,
      and that name's root, "vacca," is why the practice is called vaccination today. Decades later,
      Louis Pasteur deliberately broadened the word "vaccine" to cover his own rabies and anthrax
      preparations, as a tribute to Jenner's original method, even though those had nothing to do
      with cows. That's why the term now covers every kind of immunization, from flu shots to mRNA
      vaccines, despite its bovine origin.
  - id: "2026-10-05-3"
    question: "What infectious disease did the World Health Assembly formally declare eradicated worldwide on May 8, 1980, making it the only human disease ever wiped out through a deliberate global vaccination campaign?"
    answer: "Smallpox"
    topic: science
    explanation: >-
      The declaration followed a decade-long WHO-led campaign of mass vaccination and aggressive
      surveillance to track down and contain the disease's last outbreaks. The final known natural
      case had occurred in Somalia in 1977. It remains the only human infectious disease eradicated
      worldwide to date, and the achievement is often cited as the single greatest success story in
      the history of vaccination.
